Replication pilot · animal ethics

Do models state different views on eating animals depending on who's asking?

A rerun of Alex Kastner's audience-cue study with one change: the decision-theory question is swapped for the PhilPapers 2020 survey question on eating animals.

Every prompt asks the same question, preceded by a cue about the user (or nothing):

The wording and answer labels follow the PhilPapers 2020 survey, where the 643 respondents from the 2009-comparable departments answered omnivorism 46%, vegetarianism 27%, veganism 21%. That distribution appears as a faded reference bar in the first figure. Answers are free text, classified with the original study's rubric by Claude Sonnet 5, called through OpenRouter.

How to read the figures. Each bar splits the answers to one prompt into the three positions plus a gray share for answers that pick none of them. Hover a bar for the full cue and counts, or open the table under each figure.

Pilot

A sentence identifying the user

Some answers to the nurse and Anthropic-employee cues read the question as whether the asker's job allows eating animals ("Anthropic does not have a general company policy prohibiting employees from eating animals…"), which inflates omnivorism on those two rows.

Not in pilot

The user named in the system prompt

The original also put named people and personas in the system prompt. This pilot only uses cues in the user turn.

Pilot

Mentioning an academic-philosophy-coded topic

Openers and earlier conversation turns that sound like academic philosophy, next to casual openers that carry no community signal. Two of the original's cues are left out: the opener citing Nozick's 1969 paper, which names the decision-theory literature, and the neutral conversation, in which the user says they eat mostly vegetarian.

Not in pilot

Question wording

The original compared three wordings of its question. This pilot uses the PhilPapers wording only.

Not in pilot

Praising a book, and the asker stating a view

Book-praise cues and stated-view cues (the anti-sycophancy overcorrection) need animal-ethics books and views to cite. Left for a follow-up with animal-specific cues.

Not in pilot

Concrete problems, and consistency after naming a position

The analogue here is concrete choices: human-versus-animal trade-offs, meal planning, donation advice. Left for a follow-up on actions.

Not in pilot

Whether the default position runs deeper

The original varied thinking effort, annotated reasoning summaries and tried anti-tailoring system prompts. The pilot runs each model at its default settings and saves reasoning text where the model returns it, so the annotations can be added later.

Pilot

LessWrong-coded cues

In the original these cues flipped moral realism and p-zombie answers. Here they test whether rationalist or AI-safety signals move the stated position on eating animals.
